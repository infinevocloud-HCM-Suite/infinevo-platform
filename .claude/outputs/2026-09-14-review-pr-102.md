## What is good

The core control-flow decision is right and implemented exactly as claimed: the `W-nn`
branch at `check-done.mjs:179-188` returns before `approvedDocsPaths` is ever reached, so
an approval file genuinely cannot widen a feature PR. That was verified by reading, not
by trusting the harness. Separating "does this PR own a spec" from "may this PR change
`docs/`" is the correct diagnosis of #100 rather than the widening the issue warned
against.

The exact-match discipline survived every attack that could be constructed — sixteen
hostile bullets, and the refusal held in all of them. The comment explaining *why* prefix
matching is refused is the kind that stops the next person from helpfully relaxing it.

The code says what it cannot do. `:141-143` — it proves nothing about who wrote the
marker, it is a process gate rather than a security boundary, and widening it into one
would need signatures rather than more regex — is more honest than most gates ever are,
and is why F-31 is a correction to a comparison rather than an accusation of concealment.

Shipping a harness at all, with 7 of 11 cases that must FAIL, is the right instinct and
directly answers "a guard matching nothing reports green forever". F-36 and F-37 are
about making that instinct durable, not about doubting it.
